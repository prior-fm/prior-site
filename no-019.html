A visual model of the Moon’s uneven pull on Earth’s water, and the parts the model leaves out.

The film
Two bulges.
One lunar day.

The Moon pulls hardest on the water closest to it. On the far side, inertia helps make a second raised region. As Earth turns, coastlines pass through both.

What the model is doing
Make the
shape visible.

The water movement is intentionally much larger than a real tide. That makes the two opposing bulges legible on screen. The land stays as a visual anchor while the jade water moves around it.

This is an illustrative equilibrium model, not a coastline forecast. Real tides depend on coastlines, ocean depth, the Sun, basin shape and local conditions.
