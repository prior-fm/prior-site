The four films

A month of screen time is 240 hours, and the films count it as 240 squares, one an hour. Each part is one block of hours and four ideas: what the idea is, in one line, and the real page where you learn it, shown edge to edge in its own colours. One hour a day gets you the intuition. Eight hours a day gets you to how ChatGPT works, from the inside.

Part 1 of 4 · 30 hours Supervised. Unsupervised. Gradient descent. What training is.
Part 2 of 4 · 90 hours Loss. Overfitting. Train-test split. Evaluating a model.
Part 3 of 4 · 150 hours Forward pass. Backpropagation. Activations. Recognising a digit.
Part 4 of 4 · 240 hours Embeddings. Attention. Transformers. How ChatGPT works.
What it is
Sixteen ideas,
in order.

Machine learning is taught as a syllabus, and most people never get past the first week of one. This is not a syllabus. It is the sixteen ideas you actually need, in the order each one makes the next one obvious, and for every idea the one free place that teaches it well: Kaggle’s notebook courses, Google’s crash course, 3Blue1Brown, Nielsen’s book, Karpathy’s lectures, the Illustrated Transformer.

30 hours is supervised and unsupervised learning, gradient descent, and what training actually means. Enough to stop calling it magic. 90 hours adds loss, overfitting, the train-test split and how you tell whether a model is any good: enough to finish a first project. 150 hours goes inside a network: the forward pass, backpropagation, activations, and a small network that reads handwritten digits. 240 hours is embeddings, attention, the Transformer, and a GPT built from scratch in code.

Why the pages are in the film
Real pages,
edge to edge.

Every source in the films is shown as the actual page, captured on 2026-09-04 at phone width and scrolled the way you would scroll it, in the page’s own colours rather than Prior’s. That is deliberate. A course you have already seen the front of is a course you are more likely to open, and the point of the series is that you open one.

Every source is free to learn in full. The films say “free” and never “certified”: no provider’s own page confirmed to us that a certificate costs nothing, and the ones that do offer one either charge for it or were not confirmed. The roadmap says which is which.
