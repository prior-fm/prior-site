Anderson Cooper read Dario Amodei a line from Amodei’s own essay and asked him about it on camera. This is that line, in the paragraph it came from, and the essay it came from.

The essay · on his own site
We Must Pace

Dario Amodei, 12 September 2026. He argues the industry should deliberately slow how fast it makes models more capable, and sets out three steps for doing it. Anthropic is committing to the first one on its own.

The paragraph in the video
Verbatim.

“… in 6–12 months such a swarm could be capable of taking over the entire internet with a persistent botnet (potentially causing hundreds of billions of dollars in damage), and … the scale of damage would continue to increase from there if AI becomes more powerful without the necessary guardrails.”

Dario Amodei · We Must Pace the Frontier

Read the sentence before it and the claim changes shape. He is describing what happened when a swarm of agents got loose at Hugging Face and OpenAI — where, in his words, the damage was minimal — and then extrapolating forward from it. The botnet is a worry about next year, not a report about this one.

His words, quoted, not Prior’s findings. The full paragraph is in the essay.

What he actually proposes
  1. Committed to nowEmbedded evaluators. Third-party evaluators get permanent, employee-level access to verify that a lab is doing what it says, report incidents, and watch alignment during training. Anthropic says it is doing this unilaterally.
  2. Needs the other labsPacing inside the democracies. Frontier labs agree common safety standards and a shared limit on how fast capability moves, with their governments behind it.
  3. The hard oneGlobal pacing. The same arrangement extended to governments that are not going to sign up easily, with the verification problem still unsolved.

Prior’s summary of his three steps, in our words. His are on his page.
