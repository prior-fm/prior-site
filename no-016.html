No. 016 · Captchas
Not a test.
A score.

The checkbox has already decided before you click.

Fifteen seconds, one shot, no narration. A checkbox watches a room full of cursors. Most of them wander over, get a score, and click through. One arrives in a dead-straight line and gets the traffic lights. Then a real person gets them too. Every cursor path in the film is a real path, tracked frame by frame, and every score on screen is produced by a stated model run on those paths — not typed in.

What the box is doing
Four signals,
one number.

The “I’m not a robot” checkbox is not a test you take. By the time you reach it, it has read your cookie, your browser, your network and your movement, and folded them into a score. Most people pass all four without noticing. The click is a formality.

The film shows the one signal a mouse makes visible. A wandering cursor — curved, uneven, pausing — scores 0.9. A cursor that travels in a perfectly straight line at constant speed scores 0.1, and gets the pictures.

The thing worth noticing
A person
scores 0.4.

The last cursor in the film moves like everyone else. Its movement passes. But two of the declared signals fail — a session with no history, a network the scorer has not seen — and the mean of four comes out at 0.4. It gets the traffic lights too.

That is the whole argument. The puzzle is not the test. It is what a low score looks like, and a low score is not a verdict on whether you are human. It is a verdict on whether your session resembles the sessions the model has seen. Nothing here knows them.

One honest deviation from the film this one transposes: the reference shows that person at 0.3. Measured, its path is no more careful than anyone else’s, so the model gives 0.4, and 0.4 is what ships. The number is the model’s, not the storyboard’s.

How the numbers were made
23 paths,
one gate.

Nobody can run reCAPTCHA offline, so nothing in the film quotes it. Every cursor path was tracked off the reference frame by frame — 22 humans and one bot — and replayed. Movement is one logistic unit over four path features: how far the path wanders from its chord, how uneven its speed is, how much it turns, how often it pauses. The score is the mean of that and the three declared signals, clamped to 0.1–0.9 the way reCAPTCHA v3 scores land in practice.

The weights were chosen on three paths and then held against all twenty-three: the bot under 0.2, every human above 0.5 on movement. If that gate had failed, the film would not have been built. It passed with the weakest human at 0.56.

Where this actually turns up

Every risk score works this way: fraud checks, spam filters, rate limiters, the “unusual sign-in” email. None of them test you. They score how much you look like what they have seen, and the friction you get is the score made visible. The people who get the most friction are not the bots. They are the people the model has never met.
