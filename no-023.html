No. 023 · time to decide
Twenty
minutes.

An object crosses the border. Someone has to say shoot or wait, and the clock is already running. Five European governments and the United States published how long theirs took. This is that, as data.

The film
One dot,
one minute
of flight.

Every map in the film runs at one dot per minute, and every clock at one minute to four tenths of a second. That is the only way the times can be compared by eye: a decision that took 17 minutes and one that took 83 are the same picture at different lengths.

Two clocks, not one
They do not
agree.
The white bar · the air force From the first time a government published for an incident, to the shot. That first time is not the same kind of event everywhere — in Romania it is radar, in Estonia and Latvia it is the alert, in Lithuania it is an observation. The film says which, every time.
The grey bar · the phones When the emergency alert went out to when it was lifted. A different clock, run by a different agency, and it does not start when the first one does.
Galați · 16 August The shot was at 05:01. The RO-Alert went out at 05:08. The public’s clock started seven minutes after it was already over.

Shot time MApN. Alert time IGSU, reported ‘after the shot’ by Viața Liberă.

What each government published
The times,
with their sources.
  • Lithuania · 15 September Observed in from Belarus at roughly 00:00, shot at roughly 00:20 by Italian Eurofighters out of Šiauliai. It carried an explosive charge, later defused. Armed Forces · NKVC · R. Kaunas
  • Romania · 24 July On radar east of Sulina at 09:39. Italian jets up at 09:48, Romanian at 10:56, shot near Padina, Buzău at 11:02 — 83 minutes from radar to shot, which is our own subtraction of the two published times. MApN
  • Romania · 16 August Radar at 04:44, shot at 05:01 — 17 minutes. The fastest published decision in the film. MApN
  • Estonia · 19 May Alert sent at 12:00, shot at 12:14, all-clear around 12:55. Origin later given as ‘likely’ Ukrainian; Ukraine’s minister apologised. Kaitsevägi · mil.ee · ERR · Delfi
  • Latvia · 8 June Alert at 09:18, shot at 10:05 — 47 minutes from the alert. The entry time was not published, so there is no radar-to-shot figure to give. LETA · LRT
  • Finland · 20 August A crewed IL-20 reconnaissance aircraft, roughly 3 minutes inside, treated as a suspected violation and under investigation. No shot. The counter-example the film needs: the decision can also be not to fire. Raja.fi
  • North America · 28 January → 4 February 2023 The balloon. Authorised on Wednesday 1 February, shot on Saturday 4 February, over water, because the Department of Defense judged the debris an ‘undue risk’ over land. 10,080 minutes — seven days — compressed into eight seconds of film. AP · US DoD

Every figure above is a published time or the subtraction of two of them. The subtractions are marked OUR COUNT on screen.

What the film does not claim

The routes on the maps are schematic. They connect the places each government actually named, one dot per minute of flight, and they are not flight tracks — no one published those.

The two clocks are not interchangeable and the film never averages them. A radar-to-shot figure and an alert-to-shot figure measure different things, and where only one was published, only one is shown.

No government had published a launch point for the Lithuanian object when the film was made. The last card says so, and leaves it blank.
