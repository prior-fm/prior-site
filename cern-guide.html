BUTTER / CERN FIELD GUIDE

The particles vanished.
The clues survived.

A visual companion to the CERN film: what the machine does, what the detector records, and what the entanglement evidence means.

01 / Create the collision

The Large Hadron Collider accelerates proton beams around a 27-kilometre ring. Detectors such as ATLAS and CMS surround collision points. The accelerator creates collisions; a detector measures particles emerging from them.

Our brick constructions and control room are explanatory models, not literal CERN architecture or scale drawings.

02 / Follow what survives

A Higgs boson can decay through H → ZZ* → 4ℓ. The four charged leptons are electrons or muons. The asterisk identifies an off-shell Z. These measured decay products provide information about the parent pair’s spin correlations.

This is statistical reconstruction. Scientists do not watch two surviving Z bosons communicate.

03 / A match is not enough

Two boxes packed with matching objects illustrate ordinary correlation. Entanglement asks a more demanding question: can the joint quantum state be represented as a mixture of separate states?

One decay cannot establish this. Researchers analyze distributions across many events. The boxes and colored grids in the film are teaching analogies, not the actual ATLAS statistical model.

04 / Read the result with its limits

ATLAS and CMS report strong evidence for entanglement between Z bosons from Higgs decay. The ATLAS comparison shown in our film relies on Standard Model assumptions. It is not a demonstration of faster-than-light signalling or a loophole-free Bell test.
